# Evidence and citations

## Source path

Use:

```text
article claim
→ original URL or raw source
→ source-ledger record
→ research report for context only
```

Never cite `report.md`, `source-ledger.md`, `market-scan.md`, `topic-options.md`, or `outline.md`
as factual authorities.

## Load-bearing claims

A claim is load-bearing when changing or removing it would weaken the thesis. Verify these against
the original source immediately before drafting, especially when they involve:

- definitions and attributed positions;
- origin and chronology;
- product behavior or availability;
- benchmarks and statistics;
- popularity or performance;
- causal claims;
- quotations.

## Citation placement

- Link the source at the claim it supports.
- Prefer primary sources for hard facts.
- Use independent sources for interpretation and criticism.
- Cite social posts when the post itself is the event, definition, or discourse evidence.
- Do not attach one citation to a paragraph containing several unrelated claims.

## Uncertainty

Keep uncertainty when it matters:

- `The earliest example found in this research...`
- `The available evidence suggests...`
- `These figures are not directly comparable...`
- `The term is still used in two competing ways...`

Do not import ledger codes such as `probable` or `C-004` into the prose.

## Quotes

Quote only language whose wording matters. Paraphrase the rest and preserve attribution. Avoid
assembling the article from source phrasing even when every passage is cited.
