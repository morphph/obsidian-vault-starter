# Content analysis

Analyze content as a deliberate editorial product, not merely as a source of facts.

## Record per example

```markdown
## <title>

- **URL:**
- **Author / publisher:**
- **Published:**
- **Intended reader:**
- **Format and length:**
- **Hook:** <how attention is earned>
- **Promise:** <what the reader expects to gain>
- **Thesis / frame:** <the central claim or lens>
- **Proof mechanism:** examples | data | authority | tutorial | personal experience | analogy
- **Structure:** <the narrative sequence>
- **Depth:** introductory | practitioner | expert
- **Distinctive move:** <what this piece does unusually well>
- **Missing or weak:** <unanswered question, unsupported claim, omitted audience>
- **Response signal:** observed | proxy | unavailable
- **Observed metrics and timestamp:**
- **Useful audience comments:**
```

## Analysis discipline

- Separate a strong hook from a strong thesis.
- Separate content performance from author fame.
- Do not assume search position means reader satisfaction.
- Do not copy a competitor's title, phrasing, or signature structure.
- Use comments as evidence of reader questions, not as factual authority.
- Note when several pieces inherit the same frame from one original source.
- Compare content serving the same reader and job-to-be-done.
