# Source quality and claim discipline

## Match evidence to the claim

Use the closest available source:

1. original artifact, dataset, paper, code, transcript, or announcement;
2. official or first-party documentation;
3. independent analysis that shows its work;
4. community commentary and social posts;
5. aggregators and search snippets.

Lower-ranked sources can reveal discourse, criticism, and terminology. They should not silently
carry hard facts that a primary source could establish.

## High-risk claims

Apply a counter-search before using:

- earliest, first, original, invented by;
- only, always, never;
- best, top, most popular, fastest-growing;
- exact dates, prices, benchmarks, view counts, or market figures;
- "official" affiliations and attributed job titles;
- causal statements such as "X made Y happen".

If the search is not exhaustive, qualify the wording: `earliest traceable in this search`,
`one of the most visible`, or `a representative analysis`.

## Separate four layers

- **Observed fact:** directly visible in a source or dataset.
- **Source claim:** asserted by a source but not independently verified.
- **Researcher synthesis:** inferred by connecting multiple sources.
- **Recommendation:** a judgment about what the reader should do.

The report may contain all four, but the ledger must make the transitions visible.

## Triangulate without vote-counting

Three articles repeating one unsourced statement are one evidence chain, not three confirmations.
Trace repetitions back to their common origin. Preserve real disagreement when sources use
different definitions or measure different things.

## Freshness

For fast-moving topics:

- state an `as_of` date;
- prefer pages with visible update dates;
- record when engagement metrics were observed;
- avoid treating product docs, model names, or availability as timeless;
- put likely-to-change facts in the ledger's open questions.

## Report readability

Do not paste the ledger into the report. Use concise citations beside claims and natural language
for uncertainty. Reserve callouts for disagreements that materially change the reader's model.
