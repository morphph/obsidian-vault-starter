# Community-formed topic research

Use this reference when a term, practice, or narrative forms in a community before an official
definition exists.

## Map roles, not just channels

Find:

- **Originators** — earliest traceable articulation or naming.
- **Amplifiers** — posts or people that caused wider attention.
- **Definers** — competing attempts to explain what the term means.
- **Practitioners** — people showing real implementations or results.
- **Institutional adopters** — vendors or organizations reframing existing work under the term.
- **Critics** — people challenging novelty, usefulness, or evidence.

One person may occupy multiple roles. Record the role a specific source plays.

## Reconstruct the discourse

Answer:

1. What problem existed before the label?
2. Who named or reframed it?
3. What event made the label spread?
4. Which definitions compete?
5. What is genuinely new: the structure, the capability, the economics, or only the language?
6. Which institutions benefit from the framing?
7. What evidence would falsify the dominant story?

## Use channel-native evidence

- X and social posts: origin, amplification, concise definitions, criticism.
- GitHub: implementation reality, chronology, adoption, open issues.
- Hacker News and Reddit: practitioner skepticism, failure modes, reader questions.
- Blogs and newsletters: extended arguments and framing.
- Talks and videos: teaching patterns, demonstrations, and audience response.
- Official docs and papers: capability boundaries and hard claims.

Do not force all channels into every report.

## Popularity versus importance

A viral post may be historically important to the discourse without being factually reliable.
A low-engagement original source may be the strongest factual evidence. Describe these as separate
dimensions.

Only call content `Top` when the comparison set and observed metrics are available. Otherwise use:

- discourse-shaping;
- representative;
- widely cited in the sampled sources;
- useful explainer;
- notable criticism.

## Preserve definition drift

When a term has multiple meanings:

- quote or accurately paraphrase each definition;
- attach it to the community or author using it;
- explain overlap and incompatibility;
- state which meaning the report uses;
- do not collapse homonyms merely because they share a label.
